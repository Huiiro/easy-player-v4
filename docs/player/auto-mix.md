# Auto Mix transitions

Enable Crossfade and Auto Mix (formerly Adaptive timing). The configured overlap remains an upper bound. Fixed crossfades retain their equal-power envelope.

## Analysis and cache

The engine scans the last 60 seconds in 100ms RMS blocks, extending backwards in 30-second windows when the entire window is silent, up to 180 seconds total. Trailing silence of at least 600ms is trimmed with a 150ms guard. The threshold is max(-80 dBFS, 0.3% of peak block RMS). Silent intros are trimmed with a 50ms guard; this offset survives seeks and is included in the incoming source position. When an outro has already faded substantially, the handoff can move to the last useful energy (30% of the scanned peak), bounded by eight seconds or the configured overlap plus two seconds. This avoids transitioning only after musical energy has disappeared.

Independent local musical analysis examines up to 24 seconds before the last audible block and 24 seconds of the next intro. A 10ms onset envelope is fitted to candidate beat grids from 65 to 180 BPM. At least eight onsets, support in both halves of the region, and high grid coverage are required. Confidence below 0.78 prevents beat matching. Repeated four-beat accent grouping is used only when supported by both regions; this is not a trained downbeat or phrase-boundary detector and does not assume every track is 4/4.

Windowed Goertzel measurements build a local chroma profile. Major/minor template correlation must clear both absolute and runner-up margin thresholds; sparse or ambiguous material returns unknown key. The key is used for compatibility decisions, never automatic pitch shifting.

A thread-safe, bounded 16-entry in-memory LRU caches musical descriptors by path, file size, modification time and region. Failed analysis is not cached. Repeated queue visits reuse descriptors; file changes invalidate them. No model downloads, GPU runtime, Python runtime or new library dependency is required.

## Transition strategies

- **Beat matched:** high-confidence grids, compatible known keys (or unknown key), broadly similar handoff descriptors and tempo ratio within 0.96..1.04. Start is snapped to an outgoing beat, or supported four-beat accent boundary. Audible intro audio is preserved after silence trimming, with a delay of at most one beat to align its first beat. At least four matched beats must fit after this delay. Overlap is bounded by the setting, six seconds, and half the incoming track.
- **Staged overlap / harmonic staged:** unavailable beat matching or conflicting keys still produce an audible overlap. The nominal overlap is the configured duration, reduced by at most 25% for dissimilar material, capped at 12 seconds and half the incoming track duration. A five-second setting therefore usually gives 3.75 to five seconds instead of the previous 0.8 to 1.5 seconds. Very short requested durations (up to 500ms) remain unchanged. The key compatibility rules still govern beat matching (same key, adjacent fifths in the same mode, relative major/minor).
- **Late short:** only genuinely late preparation shortens to the remaining time. If already inside trailing silence, schedule a 50ms handoff after the next undecoded frame. Previously buffered audio is not discarded.

Adaptive overlaps preserve full-band equal-power envelopes for both sources throughout the overlap. The previous delayed midrange entrance suppressed voices together with instruments; it has been removed. A gentle bass timing correction is limited to a 110Hz low-pass band and 30-35% correction strength, retaining the unfiltered envelope for at least 65% of the bass. Bass trades near the midpoint (42-58%) without suppressing the vocal body or consonants. Filter state persists between decoder blocks and resets on plan/seek changes. This is broad frequency routing, not stem separation or reliable isolation of vocals/drums. Fixed crossfades remain broadband equal-power.

## Rendering and playback

During crossfade playback, each incoming PCM decoder converts sample rate and channel layout to the ongoing session format using swresample. Bit depth differences are accepted because mixing uses float PCM. The backend, ring buffer and shared DSP remain running across the transition. Frame timestamps account for resampler latency; seek and duration use the session sample rate. DSD and crossfade-disabled playback retain their existing format rules. Decoder-side format conversion is reported as a bit-perfect blocker even if crossfade is subsequently disabled.

The outgoing deck remains at its natural tempo. An independent incoming SoundTouch instance renders just the overlap before playback consumes it, outside the decoder lock and audio callback. It keeps pitch, matches the initial tempo, then restores the original tempo over the final beat. A short endpoint blend rejoins the original PCM exactly; the next decoder resumes at the corresponding consumed source frame. Shared playback-speed/DSP processing still runs after the mix.

Queue/settings/seek revisions invalidate stale analysis. Late preparation discards the rendered segment and uses a shorter transition. Seeking rewinds the incoming decoder for reuse. Settings changed during an audible adaptive overlap apply to subsequent transitions; the current overlap completes to avoid dropping pre-read audio.

## Limits and verification

Crossfade transitions support mixed PCM sample rates, channel counts and bit depths within one session; beat rendering currently supports mono/stereo. DSD, all-silent/malformed files and unsupported duration metadata retain fallback behavior. Audible leading intro material is preserved. Without the optional stem runtime this path does not isolate vocals. The optional stem/cue behavior is described below. No path uses trained phrase detection, makes large tempo changes, shifts key or infers half/double tempo matches.

`auto_mix_test` covers 120/123 BPM analysis, aligned rendered onsets, jitter rejection, silence, ambiguous/known key profiles, harmonic and tempo fallback, exact PCM continuation, cache invalidation, end-to-end mixed playback and timeline position, seek reuse, settings changed mid-overlap, long/quiet silent tails, late preparation and short tracks. Additional regression checks cover 44.1/48 kHz conversion in both directions, 16/24-bit changes, mono/stereo mapping, 1.5-second silent intros, conversion-frame accounting and seeking. The simulated backend inspects actual output samples and rejects silent runs of 20ms or longer in continuous synthetic material. `audio_backend_recovery_test` covers playback lifecycle and device recovery.

A real-playlist regression mode reads two UTF-8 paths from a manifest and verifies a native transition with one backend open. Reproducing the log-reported HOYO-MiX 48 kHz/24-bit to Release My Soul 44.1 kHz/16-bit pair completed without device reopening and with a measured longest zero-sample run of 0ms in the accelerated simulated backend. This measures signal continuity, not subjective seamlessness. Naturalness on real playlists still needs listening evaluation.

The audible-overlap regression captures backend PCM from 440Hz and 1760Hz sources, then measures both frequencies in 100ms windows. It requires at least one continuous second in which both sources are audible; merely eliminating zero samples cannot pass it. Additional checks verify exact band-filter endpoint reconstruction and invariance to decoder block size. A 12-second preview of a recent real-playlist transition can be generated with `auto_mix_test <UTF-8 two-path manifest> <preview.wav>`; it uses the simulated backend and never plays sound automatically.

SoundTouch tempo API reference: https://www.surina.net/soundtouch/README.html

Voice-band regression measures incoming and outgoing components at 120, 250, 500, 1000, 2000 and 3500Hz at multiple transition positions. Each must stay within 2dB of its full-band equal-power reference; it catches the former midrange suppression while the audible-overlap and continuity checks remain in place. This verifies band retention, not isolated vocal recognition.

## Optional local vocal protection

A local Demucs `htdemucs` worker now separates only the two transition regions into a vocal estimate and residual accompaniment. Each region includes up to three seconds of surrounding context; the requested overlap is at most twelve seconds. The original mixture minus the vocal estimate is used as accompaniment, so the two components reconstruct the mixture without independently normalizing stems.

When ready before the overlap begins, the worker examines up to twelve seconds of the outgoing tail and chooses the later of the last useful accompaniment (30% of local peak RMS) and last sustained vocal plus a 300ms release. Vocal-tail gating uses an absolute floor, a local-peak threshold and 10% of mixture RMS; at least four of six 100ms blocks must be active. Healthy endings are retained. Only depleted fades of at least 600ms are shortened, by at most eight seconds; the full overlap moves earlier with its sample-aligned stem. This replaces unconditional restoration of the entire natural fade, which could leave a low-energy hole when the incoming phrase also pauses. Native playback validates the bounded position and rejects results once decoding reaches the revised start. Its vocal has a separate hold/fade envelope and at most +6dB compensation, applied only when the incoming accompaniment warrants it. An 80ms smoothed vocal activity estimate, additionally gated by absolute and incoming-bed-relative audibility, lowers incoming accompaniment by up to about 9dB and competing incoming vocals by up to 6dB, recovering over the final part of the overlap. No vocal estimate means ordinary equal-power playback. This can preserve a fading final sentence, but cannot recover information already absent from the recording; source-separation artifacts remain possible.

The main process starts a hidden persistent Python worker after an 800ms queue debounce. Only the latest pending pair survives, stale results are discarded by both job ID and native plan revision, and results arriving after the overlap starts are ignored. Each task has a three-minute timeout. Stop/lightweight mode/quit cancel the worker; idle workers exit after one minute to release model memory. Errors or unavailable runtime keep the existing non-stem overlap without delaying playback. Stem estimates use file path, size, nanosecond modification time, region and model version cache keys. Audio cache is bounded to 512MiB; the model cache is separate.

Windows development setup (Python 3.11/3.12 and FFmpeg on PATH):

```powershell
powershell -File scripts/setup-auto-mix-stems.ps1
```

The installer creates `build/stem-runtime`, installs CPU PyTorch/torchaudio 2.5.1 and Demucs 4.0.1 plus its tensor-processing dependencies, and fetches the official model (about 80MiB). Demucs's older torchaudio package constraint is bypassed because this worker uses FFmpeg and direct tensor inference rather than legacy torchaudio file I/O. No system Python environment is modified. The installed environment and weights are ignored build artifacts.

Development auto-discovers that runtime and uses `build/auto-mix-stems`. Packaged builds include `resources/auto-mix/stem_worker.py`, but do not embed the large Python/PyTorch environment: supply an installed compatible runtime through `EASY_PLAYER_STEM_PYTHON` or `tools/auto-mix-python/python.exe` beneath the resource directory (on Unix, `bin/python3`). `EASY_PLAYER_STEM_FFMPEG` can specify FFmpeg. Packaged audio/model cache resides in the application cache. Model execution and all audio remain local; downloading model weights is the only required inference setup network operation.

Validation: `node scripts/test-auto-mix-stems.mjs` checks worker cancellation, stale queue results, timeout and restart with a deterministic worker. Native checks measure that a quiet 700Hz vocal component stays forward while a loud 1800Hz incoming accompaniment yields; zero-vocal reconstruction, endpoints and stale native commits are checked too. Real regions were separated in about 14-16 seconds on this CPU; a repeat cached request returned in 1.6 seconds including Python/import startup. These are local measurements, not performance guarantees. A real `set_auto_mix_vocals` playback test preserved the longer outgoing tail and kept a single backend open without zero-sample gaps. Actual vocal clarity still requires listening.

Model and API sources: https://github.com/facebookresearch/demucs and https://github.com/facebookresearch/demucs/blob/main/demucs/pretrained.py

## Enter near the next vocal

`crossfadeVocalCue` is a persisted, independently switchable option beneath Auto Mix, enabled by default. It requires the optional local stem runtime. When enabled, the worker analyzes up to the first 40 seconds after the existing silence-trimmed intro. A vocal estimate must exceed an absolute level threshold, a fraction of its local peak and 18% of mixture RMS, with sustained activity in at least four of six 100ms blocks. Noise/leakage, one short burst or no detected vocals leaves the original entry unchanged.

For a reliable entry, keep `clamp(overlap * 0.65, 1, 3)` seconds before the detected voice. This places its first vocal around the outgoing vocal release, preserving a short musical lead-in. Skip only when it removes at least one second and the complete overlap still fits; the native receiver validates the bounded cue and rejects it if the option is disabled, settings/queue changed, or the overlap is already underway. Disabling the option preserves all audible intro material while retaining vocal protection. A seek entering an overlap also advances incoming PCM to match the vocal stem offset.

The optional 40-second analysis makes initial preparation slower than the short-region-only 14-16 second measurement above. It is cached separately. On a real test the detected cue skipped 1.86 seconds, kept about 2.44 seconds before the next vocal estimate and took 21.75 seconds on first preparation. These thresholds are heuristics over a separation model, not word recognition; an uncertain or late result falls back without blocking playback. The synthetic worker tests check sustained activity, leakage/burst rejection, lead-in preservation and the disabled option; native tests check disabled-cue rejection and seek alignment.

The tail-coverage regression retains a final sustained phrase plus release, removes depleted accompaniment-only fades, ignores quiet separator leakage and preserves healthy endings or late vocals. The native vocal regression also requires a barely audible tail to release the incoming bed while keeping the existing quiet-vocal protection check. Service tests verify that both incoming cue and outgoing overlap positions reach the native commit. A real Skydive → 春めく reproduction moved the full 3.92-second overlap 2.10 seconds earlier while retaining the final vocal release and using one continuous backend session. This is a measured scheduling/coverage improvement, not a guarantee of subjective seamlessness.
